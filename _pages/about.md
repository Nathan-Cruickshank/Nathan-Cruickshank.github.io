I am a theoretical cosmologist and recent PhD graduate, specialising in numerical modelling and statistical methods. My doctoral research at the Institute of Cosmology and Gravitation, University of Portsmouth, conducted under the supervision of Professor Robert Crittenden, Professor Kazuya Koyama, and Professor Marco Bruni, focussed on models of interacting dark energy and dark matter with pure momentum exchange. Specifically, I explored how such interactions impact the late-time growth of large-scale structure in the Universe and how they might be used to resolve cosmological tensions.

To tackle these complex physics problems, I modified and utilised specialised numerical solver codes in C and Python, alongside applying advanced statistical techniques such as Markov Chain Monte Carlo, Principal Component Analysis, and Fisher forecasting.

On this website, you can find more information about my research and publications, as well as my CV.
